    Find a time
    
        
      
          
    • 
            Each person has a zone and windows on their own clock: preferred, acceptable or
            unavailable. Outside every window is unavailable. Where windows overlap, unavailable
            wins over preferred, and preferred over acceptable. A window ending at or before its
            start runs past midnight.
          
      • 
          
    • 
            Weekly windows apply on the days ticked. A date exception replaces the windows that
            start on that date; with no windows, the person is unavailable that day. The chevron
            beside a name folds or opens that person; the red bin removes them.
          
      • 
          
    • 
            The whole call must fit, within the dates searched and from now on. On a night the
            clocks change, a window in a repeated hour counts both times, and a skipped hour does
            not exist. Times that are preferred by everyone come first. Compromises then rank by the
            most minutes any one person spends outside their preferred hours, then by everyone's
            total, then the earliest. A run of starts that cost the same is one suggestion.
          
      • 
          
    • 
            Unavailable time is never used unless that person's "Allow unavailable time" is on;
            those times rank after every other. When nothing fits, the search says whose windows
            stand in the way.

            row to see them all. The timeline shows one day in the zone you choose, with each
            person's own hours above their row; the red line is now. Click or drag on it to move the
            call, or choose another suggestion; Copy summary gives everyone's own date and time.

    Convert
    
        
      
          
    • 
            Type a Unix timestamp, an ISO 8601 date (2026-10-12T14:30, with or without an offset),
            or an HTTP or email date. A number from 100,000,000,000 up is read as milliseconds,
            below that as seconds. Seconds or Milliseconds rewrites the timestamp in that unit, the
            same moment, and reads what you type next in it.
          
      • 
          
    • 
            A date or time without an offset is read in the zone you choose, and a date alone at
            00:00 there; the result says so. Where clocks go back, a time happens twice: choose
            which. Where they go forward, it never happens: it is read as the time on the clock
            after the change, and the result says so.
          
      • 
          
    • 
            Results show Unix seconds and milliseconds, UTC, this device's zone, the zone it was
            read in and your pinned zones, each with its offset at that date, plus an HTTP date and
            how long ago or ahead it is. Each has a copy button.
          
      • 
          
    • 
            Several lines convert together into a table. A line may start with a label, as in
            exp: 1760264100 or a JSON line "exp": 1760264100,. A time that
            happens twice is not guessed in a table: give it an offset.

    Duration
    
        
      
          
    • 
            From and To are each a date, with an optional time, or Now. To starts on Now: give a
            birth date for an age, or the day you met. For a countdown, switch From to Now, which
            switches To back to a date. Without a time, 00:00 in the chosen zone is assumed, and the
            result says so.
          
      • 
          
    • 
            Calendar counts whole years, months and days on the wall clock, so a day of 23 or 25
            hours at a clock change is still one day. A month too short for the day ends at its last
            day: 29 February birthdays count on 28 February in other years. From a past date to now,
            it says when that date next comes round.
          
      • 
          
    • 
            Elapsed gives the whole span in days, in hours, in minutes and in seconds, a day being
            24 hours. Completed counts only whole units; Nearest rounds to the nearest whole unit.
            It counts live, every second, while one end is now.
